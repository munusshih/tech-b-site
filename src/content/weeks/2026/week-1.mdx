For this first assignment, choose one subject that could be understood, described, or perceived as demonstrating [some form of “intelligence.”](https://www.hutter1.net/ai/idefs.pdf) Your subject might be a historical artifact, technical system, computational model, game, method of prediction, classification system, conversational script, organism, environmental process, social behavior, or contemporary AI system. The goal is not to prove that your subject is intelligent. Instead, begin by asking: what makes someone describe this system as intelligent, and what assumptions are built into that description?

Spend the week researching how your chosen subject operates. Rather than focusing only on what it is or what it represents, pay attention to what it does. What does it sense, receive, remember, compare, classify, predict, repeat, or respond to? What kinds of rules, conditions, patterns, inputs, outputs, or feedback loops allow it to function? Collect at least **five research artifacts** that help you understand the system. These may include images, diagrams, videos, manuals, texts, screenshots, technical documents, advertisements, oral histories, or other relevant materials.

From your research, create a **preliminary map** of the system using a simple structure such as **Input → Process / Rule → Output**. The diagram does not need to be polished; boxes, arrows, drawings, notes, screenshots, or other forms of representation are welcome. Accompany the map with at least five observations about how the system behaves and one question that you are still unsure about. For example: Where does the intelligence of this system actually reside? What happens when its rules encounter something unexpected? Is prediction a form of intelligence if the system does not understand what it predicts?

Bring your research, observations, and system map to class next week for a short informal presentation.
