Return to your Uncertain Archive and compare the ways you and the machine have organized it. Look at your morphology, taxonomy, and typology maps alongside the predictions and failures from your model experiments. Use **supervised and unsupervised learning as inspiration to develop a design proposal and begin prototyping it**.

What did your own categories leave out? What did the machine make visible that you had overlooked? Where do both systems seem convincing, even when they distort what you know about the collection?

Choose one of these differences and **make it the material of your work**. Use patterns, sequences, and a chosen medium to explore the relationship between human judgment and machine processing. What happens when the same collection tells two incompatible stories? How might the things that resist classification change the way the archive is experienced? What happens when the archive becomes a source for generating new things? Which patterns get repeated, exaggerated, or lost?

**Make a prototype that lets someone encounter this tension.** Try it with someone and use what happens to develop the proposal further. You can work with your existing model, explore generation, or translate the ideas into physical materials, code, images, sound, or instructions. Generation may take more technical experimentation, but you are welcome to investigate how your archive could produce new images, text, sounds, or forms. Consider how the collection and the model shape what is generated, and what assumptions are carried into the new material. Implementing an unsupervised or generative model is optional.

Write a short reflection on how your classification and the machine's processing produce meaning, where they break down, and what cultural or aesthetic assumptions they reinforce. If you work with generation, include how the outputs relate to your source material and what they reveal or distort about the archive.
