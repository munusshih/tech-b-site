After working with your Uncertain Archive, **what is your opinion about machine learning and deep learning? Develop an argument from what you have actually tried.**

Choose a claim you want to make. What did the model help you see, misunderstand, or question? Support your position with specific examples from your archive, including predictions, failures, or generated outputs. Consider where your evidence complicates your argument or leaves room for disagreement.

**Let this argument guide the project you began last week.** Develop its form so someone can experience your position through the work. Prepare a short statement of your argument and the examples behind it for your presentation.

### Prepare for Open Classroom — November 3

Next week marks the completion of Project 2 and our Open Classroom across Technology B sections. After our regular class presentations, we will open the classrooms for a mini exhibition, approximately **4:20–4:50 PM**. You will be able to walk between sections, explore your peers’ projects and have informal conversations about how others have approached classification, archives and machine learning.

Prepare your work so someone encountering it for the first time can understand what you have made and begin exploring it without a formal presentation. Bring:

- **A digital presentation:** Have your project ready on a laptop, screen or another appropriate device. Make interactive work, moving images and computational systems easy for visitors to try or view.
- **A printed poster:** Prepare a **24″ × 36″** poster with project visuals or documentation. Smaller posters or tiled prints are also welcome.
- **An exhibition label:** Include your name, project title, medium and a brief description of what you have made and explored so far. You may include this label on the poster.

Use this week’s testing to refine both the project and how you introduce it. Consider what a visitor should notice first, how they will interact with the work and what question you would like to discuss with them.
