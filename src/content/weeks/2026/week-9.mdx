In Module 1, we asked **“What is intelligence?”** and you answered by making something. This time, we have been letting machines into our archives, asking them to sort, find patterns and imagine things from what they have seen. Along the way, you may have found a connection you had missed, watched a model get something confidently wrong, or wondered why its way of seeing felt so different from yours.

**What does it mean for a machine to learn?** Bring your own answer into the work you have been developing. Start with something that stayed with you from your experiments, whether a surprise, a frustration, a strange result you keep returning to. Let it become a work that gives someone else a way into what you think about machine learning and deep learning. Your response might be curious, skeptical, playful, uneasy, or hold several feelings at once.

### Open Classroom on November 3

Next week, we will finish Project 2 by opening our doors to the other Technology B sections. After our class presentations, around **4:20 - 4:50 PM**, our classrooms will become a small exhibition. You will have time to wander, try things, meet one another and discover how differently people have approached the same questions.

This week is about getting your work ready for those encounters. Imagine someone stopping by who has never seen your archive or heard you talk about it. Give them something to notice, something to try and a reason to stay for a conversation.

Bring the following.

- **Your work, ready to encounter.** Set it up on a laptop, screen or another appropriate device so visitors can watch, listen or interact. Try the setup beforehand so you can spend your time talking with people.
- **A printed poster, 24″ × 36″.** Use images or documentation to introduce the project and share something of how you arrived there. Smaller posters and tiled prints are welcome too.
- **A short exhibition label.** Include your name, project title, medium and a few sentences about what you made. Tell visitors what caught your attention and how the work responds to it. This can be part of your poster.

Choose a few traces of your process to share alongside the work, such as an unexpected output, a comparison, a moment when you changed your mind. These can give visitors a place to begin and give you a chance to hear what someone else sees in what you have made.
