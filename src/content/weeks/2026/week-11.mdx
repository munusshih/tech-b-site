For the final project, you’ll create a speculative design prototype that reimagines NLP (Natural Language Processing) or Agentic AI beyond the dominant narratives of productivity, optimization, and acceleration. Silicon Valley often presents AI as an inevitable future where everything becomes faster, cheaper, and more efficient. But who decided these are the things technology should be good at?

What if we started with an entirely different set of values? How might AI support memory, ritual, ecological relationships, or ways of knowing that cannot easily be measured or predicted?

In Project 2, we asked what a machine could learn from our archives and what it left out. This time, start with a relationship. A language model can generate a convincing love letter, but what makes that letter meaningful to the person who receives it?

Rather than simply criticizing existing AI systems, use your project to propose an alternative or even adversarial relationship with technology, one that contests the status quo while making room for desire, ambiguity, care, and collective imagination.

The medium is open. Your project might take the form of an interface, publication, installation, artifact, performance, or something else entirely. It does not need to solve a problem or make AI more useful. Instead, consider what other futures become possible when we challenge the values built into technology.

This week, conceive a speculative project. Who is this for? Could I name them, describe them, or only imagine them? Choose one of these options.

- Something to give to a specific person
- Something to sell to a public you only partly know
- Something to keep for yourself

### To give

Design a gift for a specific person you actually know in real life.

Marcel Mauss found that a gift sustains a relationship into the future. It may also signal obligation between the parties.

The gift must actually be given. You may give it before or after critique. Either way, the giving is part of the work and we will discuss it as such

### To sell

Design something to sell to a body of people you only partly know.

According to Mauss, in contrast with a gift, a commodity produces a transaction rather than an ongoing relationship. Buyer and seller owe each other nothing further and may never meet again. At the same time, a commodity also reaches people a gift cannot.

To make something to sell, you have to imagine people you have not met: what they want, what they will understand without you there to explain it, what they will pay. A market can be computed through demographics, search terms, and purchase histories. The actual real life person who buys the thing cannot be computed.

### To keep

Design something you will keep. This is the relation to yourself. You are partly uncomputable to yourself: you cannot fully specify what you want, which is one reason to make things at all. One of life’s greatest challenges is to know what you actually want.

Part of this objective is figure out how you want to represent yourself. This work is not going into a diary. We’re going to talk about it in class. You decide how much of your introspection you want to show in critique. That decision is part of the work.
