## Lectures

In language models, **chain of thought** refers to a sequence of intermediate reasoning steps generated before an answer. In [chain-of-thought prompting](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.11903), examples show the model how to work through a question in steps. This can help with tasks such as arithmetic, where arriving at an answer involves several connected operations.

A sequence of convincing sentences can still contain a mistake. How do we decide whether the steps make sense? What makes an explanation feel trustworthy, and what would we need to check for ourselves?

We’ll begin with this idea and then look at the larger systems built around language models. A program might retrieve a passage, ask a model to use a tool, or carry information into another conversation. These are different processes from chain-of-thought prompting, but each introduces decisions about how a response takes shape. As designers, what do we choose to connect, what do we let the system decide, and where should someone be able to intervene?
